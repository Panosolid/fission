### How a boolean works here (every substep builds one piece of this)

1. Common frame. B is brought into A's local frame (A's placement is kept for the result; B gets A⁻¹ ∘ B, a pure shift when the two are turned the same way, the common case of sketches extruded alike), then both are re-centred on the middle of the overlap of their boxes, so geometry is intersected near its own origin (kernel/CLAUDE.md, "Supported modelling range").
2. Facts, decided once. Every relation between the two bodies is measured once and written in one table (`boolean/facts.rs`): each vertex of one body against each nearby face of the other (above, on or below its plane; if on, inside the face, on which edge, on which vertex, or outside), each edge against each nearby face (where it crosses), and every point that two of these produce, merged into one shared vertex. Every later step reads this table and never measures again. This is what makes the result consistent: the classic boolean bug is one routine deciding a point is on a face while another decides it is not.
3. Section edges. Where a face of A crosses a face of B, the piece of their crossing inside both faces becomes a new edge, shared by both. For planes its ends always come from the table (an edge of one face crossing the other's plane, or a vertex lying on it); for curved faces (37 on) the whole crossing curve is computed exactly and trimmed at the table's points.
4. Splitting. Every edge is cut at the shared vertices on it, and every face is cut along the section edges inside it, into pieces (a planar arrangement in the face's own 2D coordinates, uv for curved faces).
5. Classification. Each piece is IN, OUT, ON-same or ON-opposite the other body (B0 decision 7; Laidlaw, Trumbore and Hughes 1986). A piece touching a section edge is decided by the shape of the other body right at that edge (its wedge of material there); its neighbours inherit the answer across edges that were not cut; a whole shell that touches nothing is decided by a seeded ray. Two answers for one region that disagree are a bug and an error, never a vote.
6. Selection. Union keeps A OUT, B OUT, ON-same once; subtract keeps A OUT, B IN turned inside out, ON-opposite once; intersect keeps A IN, B IN, ON-same once.
7. Stitching. Kept pieces are joined by the edge objects they already share (no gluing by distance), into shells; positive shells are bodies, negative ones voids inside the smallest positive shell holding them (B0 decision 3).
8. Tidying and checks (35). Neighbouring faces on one surface merge, edges on one line join; an edge or vertex used non-manifoldly gives `NonManifoldResult` (decision 2); a face thinner than MIN_FEATURE or an edge shorter gives an error naming it (decision 6).
9. History. For every output face and edge, the input faces and edges it came from, and how (kept, split, merged, made by the crossing), which Design turns into names (40a).
10. Every output body goes through `validate::checked`.

### Rules every substep follows

1. Three bands, no fourth (makes decisions 2 and 6 precise; for B0r). Two things within their tolerance of each other are the same thing (one vertex, one plane, ON). Further apart than their tolerance but closer than MIN_FEATURE: the result would have a sliver, a wall or gap thinner than 1 µm, or an edge shorter, and the boolean returns an error naming where (with the overlay), with a message to line the parts up or move them at least 1 µm apart. At least MIN_FEATURE apart: an ordinary result. So nothing is ever silently snapped, and a near miss is never a coin flip. "A face narrower than MIN_FEATURE" means the profile rule applied to each face of the result: any two of a face's boundary pieces that share no corner are at least MIN_FEATURE apart (`ops/profile.rs`, `check_profile`), and every edge is at least MIN_FEATURE long. Where the bands are judged (B0r, 2026-10-23): the facts table knows only two answers, "the same" (within tolerance) and "apart, on this side"; the middle band is judged on the result, face by face and edge by edge (decision 6), never on the inputs. So a cut whose far wall lies 0.5 µm outside the body it cuts is fine (nothing thin is left), while a union of two boxes offset by 50 nm fails on the 50 nm step it would leave. "Within tolerance" of two entities means within the sum of their two tolerances (their tolerance balls touch); a face has no tolerance of its own, so a vertex against a plane uses the vertex's. Two faces of the result meeting at any dihedral angle, however small, are a valid knife edge, not a sliver: the profile rule lets boundary pieces that share a corner come as close as they like, and validation must accept two faces lying within tolerance of each other near their shared edge.
2. One authority per decision (architecture review item 9; for B0r). Every "is this on that" question is decided by the tolerance test in the facts table, once. Exact predicates (`robust`) are used only to order or side things the table has already separated by more than their tolerance, where rounding (about 2e-10 mm at 1 km) is some 50,000 times smaller than the gap, so the two can never disagree; a debug assertion checks they agree on every call in tests. The same rule downstream (B0r): the face arrangement (32c) and the classification (33b) never make a vertex or measure a distance of their own. If two edges of one face's planar graph cross, or come within tolerance of each other, at a point the table does not know, that is `InvalidResult` (rule 4), not a new vertex. For that to hold, the table must hold every near-touch: where an edge crosses a face, the crossing point is also placed in the face's 2D coordinates with tolerance (inside, on which edge, on which vertex, or outside), exactly as a vertex is, so an edge of one body passing within tolerance of an edge of the other is a fact ("on edge e"), not a surprise later.
3. Per-entity tolerance, always read (decision 5; architecture review item 4). Boolean code never reads LINEAR; it reads each vertex's and edge's own tolerance and never assumes it is below the 100 nm cap. A new vertex or edge may grow to what it measured, up to max(cap, the largest tolerance of the inputs it was computed from), and past that the boolean returns an error. Our own bodies are all within the cap. Imported inputs (B0r, 2026-10-23): a boolean accepts an input whose vertices and edges carry tolerances up to MIN_FEATURE / 2 (500 nm, five times the cap), and refuses an input with any entity looser than that with `InvalidInput` naming the entity ("too loose for exact booleans; heal it when importing"). The reason is the bands: two entities are "the same" within the sum of their tolerances, so two looser than half of MIN_FEATURE could be the same thing and yet a legal feature apart, and the middle band would vanish. Nothing is loosened by this: the default and the cap for our own geometry stay LINEAR and 10 × LINEAR; the limit only says what step 71's import must heal a file down to (72) before booleans will take it. The harness feeds bodies with tolerances up to 500 nm (`fixtures::tolerant`, 31a) and checks that 550 nm (1.1×) is refused by the boolean, not by `validate`, with that message.
4. Never patch. A dangling section edge, a loop that won't close, two classifications that disagree, a ray that can't find a clean path after its retries: each returns `KernelError::InvalidResult` with a failure dump (`debug_dump.rs`), panics in test builds, and becomes a regression test. Nothing is nudged, healed or dropped to make a result pass.
5. Determinism. The same inputs give the same output bit for bit, whatever order faces are stored in. For that, "a fixed order" can never mean arena order (B0r): the boolean first sorts each body's vertices, edges and faces by a geometric key (coordinates, then plane coefficients, compared lexicographically, with exact ties impossible in a valid body), every loop then runs in that order, merged vertices take the position of the best-conditioned computation (ties by the same key, never by index), ray directions come from a fixed seeded list, and the output arenas are built in that order too. Checked by building each case twice with shuffled input arenas and comparing the outputs bit for bit.
6. Bounded cost. Face pairs are found by sorting boxes (sweep and prune), so work grows with the pairs that really overlap; every loop has a cap from the input size, and every cap inside the kernel is a count of steps, never a clock, so a result never depends on the machine it ran on; `tests/worst_case.rs` gains the costliest legal boolean (two 2,000-corner plates) with a time budget, and the app's watchdog (34d), the only wall-clock limit, catches anything that still hangs.
7. Every substep starts from its line below, ends with its tests, CHANGELOG and VERSION, and ticks its line here. A kernel substep runs Tier 1, mutation testing on its new files (survivors killed or listed), joins the stateful harness the day an operation lands, and dispatches a "Random testing" run after its push (kernel/CLAUDE.md).

### Behaviour questions, answered (best end product; noted so no thread re-decides them)

- Bodies nearly lining up (between their tolerance and 1 µm): an error naming where, never a silent snap (rule 1).
- The result keeps the target's placement; the tool is moved into it (step 1).
- Combine takes several tools, applied one at a time in the order picked (as Fusion lists them); Keep tools is off by default (Fusion's default).
- A Combine Cut that would leave nothing, or an Intersect with no shared volume (bodies apart or only touching): the feature fails with words saying why and both bodies stay as they were (Fusion checks F3 and its Intersect variant: "Compute Failed", nothing changed). An Extrude cut that would remove a whole body is treated the same way.
- A Join of bodies that don't touch: no error and no warning; the result is one body per separate piece, the target's piece keeping its id and the tool's becoming a new body (Fusion check F2: Body1 and a new Body3).
- Faces from two features merged by a join answer to both names; the target's is the one shown.
- Coplanar faces before 35a and curved faces before 37/38a are refused with a message naming them, never approximated.
- Added by B0r (2026-10-23): a Combine whose target is also one of its tools is refused with `InvalidInput`. A cut or join whose result would leave two pieces touching only along an edge or at a corner fails with `NonManifoldResult` naming the place (decision 2, confirmed by the owner), even though each piece alone would be manifold; the roadmap's "keep as separate bodies" option can lift that later (Fusion check F7, queued). An input looser than MIN_FEATURE / 2 is refused naming the entity (rule 3). Two faces may meet at any dihedral angle (rule 1).
